Community starter kit

Three templates from the Network and community path, ready to copy and adapt. They are starting points for your judgment, not scripts: the path's own note still applies, growth, conflict and burnout need people, not templates.

This kit does not run a community, message members, or organize events for you. Nothing you type or copy here is sent anywhere; the page is static.

1. First-meeting agenda (about 60 minutes)

  1. Why this group exists (10 min). The organizer says in two minutes what the group is for and who it is for. Then each person says their name and what brought them.
  2. What each person wants (15 min). Go around: one thing you hope to get, one thing you can give. Someone writes both lists where everyone can see them.
  3. Shape (15 min). Decide together: how often you meet, where (the chat you already have), and what a meeting looks like. Fewer promises kept beats more promises dropped.
  4. First small thing (15 min). Pick one activity doable before the next meeting and who does it. End with the date of the next meeting.
  5. Close (5 min). One sentence each: what would make you come back.

Reading behind it: Open Source Guides: Building Welcoming Communities and the Community Canvas themes of purpose and people.

2. Welcome-and-first-action checklist for a new member

  • A named person (not "the group") says welcome within a day of them joining, and answers the first question publicly so others learn too.
  • They get three pointers in one message: what the group is for, where things live (chat, files, schedule), and the one norm that matters most.
  • They get a first action sized to finish in under an hour, with a named person to show it to when done.
  • Their first contribution, however small, gets a specific thank-you that says what was useful about it.
  • After two weeks, someone asks them one question: what was confusing when you arrived? The answer goes into fixing the welcome, not into judging them.

Reading behind it: Open Source Guides: Building Welcoming Communities and the Community Tool Box: Increasing Participation and Membership.

3. Member-benefits versus sponsor-benefits worksheet

Fill one row per offer before you ask anyone for anything. If a cell is empty, the offer is not ready.

  • What members get (specific, not "exposure"): …
  • Proof members got it last time (photo, quote, number): …
  • What the sponsor gets (audience, hiring pipeline, goodwill, something measurable): …
  • Proof you can deliver that (attendance, demographics, past sponsor outcome): …
  • What you are asking for (exact amount, items, or venue, and by when): …
  • What the sponsor is never promised (member data, speaking slots members did not choose, endorsements): …

Reading behind it: NSW Government: Finding sponsors for your event and the path stage "Benefits for each group, with proof".
